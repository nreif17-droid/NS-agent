# Dysregulation: Causes and Effects

> Source-quality key: 🟢 strong 🟡 moderate 🔴 weak/fringe/contested — see `foundations.md` §0 for full legend.

"Dysregulation" here means an ANS/HPA pattern that's chronically skewed toward sympathetic dominance, chronically blunted/shut-down, or unstable/reactive — not a clinical diagnosis. See the safety-flag rule in `README.md`.

## 1. Causes

### 1.1 Chronic stress / allostatic load — 🟢
Bruce McEwen and Eliot Stellar's **allostatic load** model (1993) frames chronic stress as cumulative "wear and tear" from repeatedly activating the SAM axis (catecholamines) and HPA axis (cortisol) to handle real or perceived threats. The cost isn't the stress response itself — it's the *recalibration* of baseline physiology that happens when the response doesn't fully stand down between stressors. This is the mechanistic backbone for why single acute stressors are usually fine but repeated/unresolved stress produces lasting effects. ([Allostatic load, Wikipedia summary of McEwen's model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allostatic_load), [scoping review of interventions — PMC7841966](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7841966/))

### 1.2 Poor sleep — 🟡 (evidence more nuanced than commonly assumed)
Poor sleep is broadly associated with ANS dysregulation, but the specific mechanism is less clean than popular framing suggests:
- A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis found **no significant difference in resting cortisol** between acute total sleep deprivation and normal sleep across most crossover studies. 🟡
- However, sleep deprivation does alter the ANS/cortisol *response to subsequent acute stressors* — sometimes blunted, sometimes exaggerated, described in the literature as a "bidirectional multi-system reactivity" pattern rather than one consistent direction. 🟡
- HRV reliably drops with sleep deprivation. 🟢

**Where sources disagree:** popular wellness content often states "poor sleep raises cortisol" as settled fact; the actual meta-analytic evidence for that specific claim (resting cortisol after acute deprivation) is weak/null. What's better supported is that poor sleep degrades *stress reactivity and recovery*, not necessarily baseline cortisol. ([Systematic review PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38991306/), [HRV meta-analysis — PMC12394884](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12394884/))

### 1.3 Trauma — 🟢 (clinical evidence strong; mechanism-of-action details contested, see Polyvagal flag in foundations.md)
PTSD is associated with measurably reduced vagal control of heart rate under mental challenge versus non-traumatized controls, and with a broader pattern of dysregulated arousal modulation — alternating hyperarousal (sympathetic overdrive) and hypoarousal states. This is well-documented clinically. Van der Kolk's specific *neuroanatomical* framing (leaning on Polyvagal Theory) carries the same contested-mechanism caveat noted in `foundations.md`. ([PMC4914824 — dysregulation of autonomic cardiac control in traumatic stress](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4914824/), [Van der Kolk, neurobiology of childhood trauma](https://therapeuticcare.ie/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/The-neurobiology-of-childhood-trauma-and-abuse.pdf))

⚠️ **This agent does not diagnose or treat trauma/PTSD.** Described trauma history or dissociative symptoms route to the safety flag (see README) — this is educational context, not a trauma-processing tool.

### 1.4 Overstimulation / sensory overload — 🟡
Directly relevant peer-reviewed literature specific to "overstimulation" as a lay-term is thin; the closest solid evidence is the stress/cognition literature already cited in `foundations.md` §4 (sympathetic/HPA activation impairing prefrontal function) plus sensory-processing research in clinical populations (autism, ADHD) that doesn't fully generalize to the general population. Treat "overstimulation causes dysregulation" as **plausible and consistent with known mechanisms, but not independently well-studied as its own category** in healthy adults. 🟡

### 1.5 Inflammation — 🟢
Inflammation and ANS activity are bidirectionally linked via the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway (see `foundations.md` §2): low vagal tone is associated with higher inflammatory markers, and systemic inflammation itself can activate vagal afferents and alter mood/cognition (sickness behavior). This is one of the better-established causal loops in this file. ([Cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763422001117))

### 1.6 Toxin / EMF exposure — 🔴 flagged, evidence weak/inconclusive
This is the one cause in the original brief that needed the most scrutiny:
- WHO has run a structured research program on radiofrequency EMF (RF-EMF) health effects since 2018, covering cognitive impairment and non-specific symptoms among its six priority topics.
- Current systematic reviews describe evidence for RF-EMF effects on cognition, symptoms, or the nervous system as **"inadequate or low"** — not "proven absent," but not supportive of the strong claims common in wellness content either. 🔴
- **This agent's stance:** EMF exposure will **not** be presented as an established dysregulation cause. If a user's intake mentions EMF concerns, the agent should note the evidence is unresolved rather than validating or dismissing it outright, and should not generate protocols premised on EMF mitigation as physiologically necessary. General "toxin exposure" (environmental pollutants, heavy metals) has a much larger and more specific toxicology literature that is out of scope for this general-wellness knowledge base and would need dedicated, condition-specific research if a user's situation calls for it — flag rather than improvise. ([WHO RF-EMF systematic review program — PMC9159629](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9159629/))

### 1.7 Blood sugar swings — 🟢 (bidirectional, not one-directional)
The relationship runs both ways: sympathetic activation raises blood glucose (via suppressed insulin + increased glucagon secretion, plus cortisol-driven gluconeogenesis), **and** glucose/insulin dynamics feed back onto autonomic activity — e.g., sympathetic nerve fluctuation around wake-time correlates with daily glycemic variability. So "blood sugar swings cause dysregulation" and "dysregulation causes blood sugar swings" are both supported; treat it as a loop, not a one-way cause. 🟢 ([American Diabetes Association journal](https://diabetesjournals.org/diabetes/article/75/1/5/163851/Neural-Regulation-of-Blood-Glucose-in-Acute-Stress), [ScienceDirect — glycemic variability and SNS](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168822718319454))

### 1.8 Isolation — 🟢
John Cacioppo's evolutionary theory of loneliness: perceived social isolation triggers a biological threat response that's adaptive short-term (heightened vigilance) but maladaptive when sustained — chronic loneliness produces stress-hormone, immune, and cardiovascular effects comparable in scale to smoking or obesity as mortality risk factors, per Cacioppo's synthesis. This is one of the more robust cause categories in this file, replicated across brain imaging, immune, and behavioral measures. 🟢 ([Nature Neuropsychopharmacology systematic review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-021-01058-7), [PMC4391342](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4391342/))

## 2. Effects

| Effect | Mechanism (best-supported) | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|
| Anxiety / emotional reactivity | Sustained catecholamine/cortisol elevation + reduced PFC regulation of amygdala | 🟢 |
| Brain fog / poor focus | Acute stress hormones take PFC "offline" relative to subcortical circuits; chronic stress causes PFC dendritic atrophy | 🟢 |
| Fatigue | HPA axis dysregulation (flattened cortisol curve), poor sleep architecture, allostatic load | 🟢 |
| Gut issues | Vagal/gut-brain axis disruption, stress-driven dysbiosis, altered GI motility & visceral sensitivity | 🟢 |
| Poor recovery (physical) | Elevated sympathetic tone blunts parasympathetic-dependent repair processes; poor slow-wave sleep reduces recovery | 🟢 |
| Emotional reactivity | Same PFC/amygdala mechanism as anxiety; low vagal tone correlates with lower emotion-regulation capacity | 🟢 |

## Flagged claims summary (for quick reference)

- 🔴 **EMF exposure as a dysregulation cause** — evidence rated "inadequate/low" by WHO's own systematic review program. Do not present as established.
- 🔴 **Polyvagal Theory's specific neuroanatomical mechanism** — contested; usable as descriptive vocabulary only, not as validated mechanism (see `foundations.md`).
- 🟡 **Sleep deprivation → elevated resting cortisol** — popular claim, weak/null in meta-analysis. Better-supported claim: sleep deprivation alters stress *reactivity*, not necessarily resting cortisol.
- 🟡 **"Overstimulation" as an independent dysregulation cause** — plausible via known stress mechanisms, but thin direct evidence in general (non-clinical) populations.

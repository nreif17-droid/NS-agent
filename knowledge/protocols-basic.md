# Protocols — Basic Tier

Situational protocols for common states, built from the generic-tier tools plus state-specific sequencing. These assume no acute medical/psychiatric crisis (see `README.md` safety flag — route out before applying any of this).

> Source-quality key: 🟢 strong 🟡 moderate 🔴 weak/fringe — see `foundations.md`.

## 1. Acute stress spike

**State markers:** racing heart, tight chest, racing thoughts, sudden overwhelm.

**Sequence:**
1. Physiological sigh × 1–3 cycles (fastest-acting acute down-regulator; 🟢 see `protocols-generic.md`).
2. Sensory/attentional grounding (5-4-3-2-1 or feet-on-floor) if thoughts are still racing after step 1. 🟡
3. If time allows, 2–5 min of slow-paced breathing (box breathing or 5s-in/5s-out) to consolidate the down-shift. 🟢

**Why this order:** physiological sigh is the fastest mechanical lever on arousal (Stanford RCT: largest same-day mood/respiratory-rate effect of the options tested); grounding addresses the *cognitive* loop (rumination/threat-appraisal) that breath alone doesn't always interrupt; sustained slow breathing consolidates the shift via vagal/HRV pathways. This sequencing is this agent's own synthesis of the individually-cited mechanisms — not itself a studied protocol as a bundle. 🟡 (bundle) / 🟢 (components)

## 2. Pre-sleep wind-down

**State markers:** approaching bedtime, need to downshift from day-mode.

**Sequence:**
1. Light/screen reduction 30–60 min before bed — mechanism now sourced (closed 2026-08-05, see below). 🟢 mechanism / 🟡 downstream intervention efficacy
2. Slow-paced breathing (5–10 min, extended exhale) — shifts ANS toward parasympathetic dominance, consistent with the sleep/ANS coupling described in `foundations.md` §5. 🟢
3. No hard-training or cold exposure in this window (see `protocols-advanced.md` — both are sympathetic activators, wrong direction for wind-down). 🟢

**Mechanism (step 1), sourced:** intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells (ipRGCs), maximally sensitive to short-wavelength ("blue," ~460–480nm) light, signal the suprachiasmatic nucleus to suppress pineal melatonin release. Evening screen/blue-light exposure measurably suppresses melatonin, delays circadian phase, and increases sleep-onset latency — this part of the chain is solid, well-replicated physiology. 🟢 ([mechanism review — Frontiers/PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12668929/), [blue-enriched light and melatonin in children — PMC6295443](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6295443/))

**Where sources disagree — the intervention, not the mechanism:** the mechanism above is settled, but evidence for *specific interventions* (blue-light-blocking glasses, blanket "no screens before bed" advice) actually improving sleep outcomes is weaker and mixed. A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis of blue-light-blocking glasses found modest effects on actigraphic sleep outcomes; a Cochrane-reviewed evidence base on evening technology use more broadly is described as inconclusive, with roughly half of trials showing benefit and half not. 🟡 **Practical implication:** frame step 1 as "reduces a known physiological input to delayed sleep onset," not as a guaranteed fix — individual response varies, and glasses/blockers are not a proven substitute for actually reducing exposure. ([blue-light glasses meta-analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2025.1699303/full), [evening technology use RCT protocol, notes mixed prior evidence](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12857899/))

## 3. Morning activation

**State markers:** grogginess, need to shift into alert/functional state.

**Sequence:**
1. Light exposure (ideally outdoor/natural light, especially short-wavelength/blue-enriched) — anchors circadian phase and drives the cortisol awakening response (CAR). Sourced (closed 2026-08-05, see below). 🟢
2. Brief inhale-emphasized or cyclic faster-paced breathing — deliberately using sympathetic activation for functional alertness. Sourced, smaller evidence base than the calming techniques (see below). 🟡
3. Hydration + protein-forward first meal — supports blood-sugar stability across the morning (see `dysregulation-causes.md` §1.7 on the glucose/ANS loop). 🟡

**Mechanism (step 1), sourced:** the CAR — a sharp cortisol rise in the first hour after waking — is enhanced by short-wavelength (blue) light exposure specifically; RCT evidence found significantly larger CAR after blue-enriched morning light versus dim-light control. This is the same photoreceptor pathway as the evening mechanism above, running in the opposite useful direction in the morning. 🟢 ([awakening effects of blue-enriched morning light — Nature Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-36791-5), [short-wavelength light and CAR in sleep-restricted adolescents — PMC3413982](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3413982/)) — one caveat worth naming: the circadian system's own endogenous rhythm also strongly modulates CAR magnitude independent of light, so light exposure is a lever, not the sole driver. 🟡 ([circadian modulation of CAR — PMC9669756](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9669756/))

**Mechanism (step 2), sourced — previously an unfilled gap:** breathing techniques that emphasize a *longer inhale relative to exhale* (the inverse of the calming extended-exhale pattern in `protocols-generic.md` §1) measurably favor sympathetic activation — during inhalation, vagal outflow is inhibited, and prolonging that phase shifts the balance toward alertness rather than calm. This is a real, if smaller and newer, area of study relative to the extended-exhale literature — described in the literature itself as intended "to promote healthy levels of alertness and mental arousal," which is exactly this protocol's use case. 🟡 ([paced breathing with prolonged inspiration increases sympathetic activity — bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.02.23.581710.full.pdf)) Cyclic hyperventilation-based techniques produce a stronger version of the same effect but carry the safety profile flagged in `protocols-advanced.md` §3 (breath-hold/blackout risk) — **not appropriate for this generic morning-activation use case**; keep morning activation to brief inhale-emphasized paced breathing, not hyperventilation-based work.

## 4. Post-conflict reset

**State markers:** after an argument or confrontation — residual sympathetic activation, replaying the interaction, emotional reactivity.

**Sequence:**
1. Physiological sigh or extended-exhale breathing to bring baseline arousal down before attempting any cognitive processing (matches `foundations.md` §4 — prefrontal function is impaired while sympathetic/HPA activation is elevated, so reflection attempted too early tends to be lower quality). 🟢 (mechanism) / 🟡 (this specific sequencing claim)
2. Delay analysis/decision-making ("don't decide anything for 20 minutes") until physiological arousal has measurably settled — directly follows from the PFC-offline mechanism above. 🟡
3. Optional: brief walk — movement supports catecholamine clearance; general exercise-physiology consensus, not independently re-verified this pass. 🟡

## 5. Focus / deep work prep

**State markers:** need sustained attention, currently scattered or under-aroused.

**Sequence:**
1. Confirm baseline arousal is adequate (not still dysregulated from something else — if so, run the acute-stress-spike sequence first).
2. Brief structured breathing (box breathing, 2–3 min) to stabilize before task onset — evidence base same as `protocols-generic.md` §1. 🟢
3. Environmental control (reduce interruption sources) — outside this agent's physiology evidence base; standard productivity practice, not sourced from the nervous-system literature in this knowledge base.

# Protocols — Generic Tier

Universal, safe-for-anyone baseline practices. No contraindication screening needed beyond the standing safety flag (see `README.md`) — these are low-risk for the general adult population. Cite `foundations.md` / `dysregulation-causes.md` for mechanism detail; this file is the practice layer.

> Source-quality key: 🟢 strong 🟡 moderate 🔴 weak/fringe — see `foundations.md`.

## 1. Extended-exhale / slow breathing

**What:** Any breathing pattern with exhale longer than inhale, paced around 5–6 breaths/minute.

**Why it works (mechanism):** Longer exhalation increases vagal (parasympathetic) outflow via respiratory sinus arrhythmia; slow-paced breathing at ~0.1Hz (≈6 breaths/min) also happens to be near most people's cardiovascular resonance frequency, producing the largest HRV amplitude swings. 🟢

**Evidence:** RCT evidence supports slow-paced breathing for increasing HRV (RMSSD) acutely, and — via the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway — for reducing circulating IL-6 in clinical populations over a multi-week intervention. ([Slow deep breathing modulates cardiac vagal activity — PMC8514507](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8514507/), [SPB + HRV-biofeedback RCT on inflammatory cytokines](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016503272300109X)) 🟢

**Two specific patterns, evidence-differentiated:**
- **Physiological sigh (double inhale, long exhale):** Stanford RCT (Balban, Huberman, Spiegel et al., 2023; Cell Reports Medicine; n=108) found 5 min/day of cyclic sighing for 4 weeks beat box breathing and mindfulness meditation on mood improvement and respiratory-rate reduction. Best-supported *fastest-acting* option for an acute spike. 🟢 ([study summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-athletes-way/202301/how-longer-exhalations-and-cyclic-sighing-make-us-feel-good))
- **Box breathing (4-4-4-4):** Also improves mood/lowers respiratory rate in the same Stanford trial, but underperformed cyclic sighing head-to-head; one later study found 6 breaths/min paced breathing outperformed box breathing specifically for post-exercise cardiovascular recovery. Still solid, just not the top performer in direct comparisons. 🟢

**How to do it:**
- *Physiological sigh:* two inhales through the nose (second one short, "topping off" the lungs) → one long, slow exhale through the mouth. Repeat 1–3 cycles for acute use, or 5 min/day for cumulative effect.
- *Box breathing:* inhale 4s → hold 4s → exhale 4s → hold 4s. Repeat for 2–5 min.

## 2. Grounding / orienting (physical + attentional)

**What:** Simple sensory-orienting practices — feeling feet on the floor, naming 5 things you can see/4 can touch/3 can hear, etc.

⚠️ **Flag — distinguish two different "groundings":**
1. **Attentional/sensory grounding** (5-4-3-2-1, feeling feet on floor) — this is standard clinical practice for down-regulating acute distress; it works via attentional redirection away from threat-appraisal loops, not a claimed electrical mechanism. Reasonably supported as a clinical technique, though much of its evidence base is practice-based rather than tightly RCT'd. 🟡
2. **"Earthing"/"grounding" (direct skin contact with the earth, claimed electron transfer)** — this is the pseudoscience-adjacent version and is **excluded from this agent's protocols**. Evidence review: small sample sizes, inconsistent controls, no established mechanism for how minuscule electron transfer would override the body's internal homeostatic systems; categorized as pseudoscientific by parts of the medical community. 🔴 ([Science-Based Medicine](https://sciencebasedmedicine.org/earthing-update/), [evidence review](https://biologyinsights.com/is-grounding-a-hoax-a-scientific-look-at-the-evidence/))

**This agent uses "grounding" only in sense (1).** If a user asks about earthing mats/barefoot-on-grass-for-electron-transfer specifically, the agent should say the electrical-transfer mechanism is not well supported, while noting the *practice* of being outside/barefoot is low-risk and may help via other well-established channels (sensory grounding, sunlight/circadian exposure, movement).

## 3. Posture

**What:** Upright, open chest posture during stress.

**Mechanism note:** Mechanical link between posture and ANS is plausible (chest expansion affects breath mechanics, which affects HRV) but the direct evidence base specific to "posture alone regulates the nervous system" is thin compared to breath and cold-exposure literature. Treat as a *complementary* low-cost practice, not a primary lever. 🟡

## 4. Hydration

**What:** Adequate fluid/electrolyte intake.

**Mechanism note:** Dehydration is a physiological stressor (reduces blood volume, raises heart rate for the same workload, can elevate cortisol in more extreme cases) — well-established in exercise physiology, less specifically studied as a standalone "nervous system regulation" lever outside of correcting actual dehydration. Include as baseline hygiene, not as an active regulation technique. 🟡
